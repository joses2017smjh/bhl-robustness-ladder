The first successful terrain execution smoke completed 24/24 episodes after fixing an output-receipt collision. The initial failed smoke is retained as a software-execution failure and excluded from scientific counts. That correction changed evidence writing, not control parameters or qualification thresholds. A subsequent full shard stopped after seven episodes when an extremely sparse scan failed the native timestamp-span guard. The original confirmation cohort is therefore incomplete. A separately frozen acquisition adapter records and drops invalid raw scans while preserving their original timestamps, IMU coverage and the existing stale-pose stop. Its replacement confirmation uses fresh reset seeds and a fresh smoke; superseded outcomes cannot enter the new cohort.

The native camera/fault smoke completed all 14 episodes. Under the fixed new startup excitation, the stereo–IMU arm produced 38 initialized/accepted frames. All five nominal arms passed readiness; no full navigation improvement is inferred from these short trials. One orchestration job needed an explicit Python 3.11 interpreter; its failed pre-submission attempt is retained without rerunning scientific trials.

The external TUM VI replay processed 1,200 real stereo pairs in each arm and 11,956 genuine IMU samples in the inertial arm. Stereo tracked all 1,200 pairs. Stereo–IMU provided 1,027 initialized tracked poses and reset its active map once, reusing the same map ID. The corrected evaluator splits that reset into separate coordinate epochs. On the main epoch's 1,008 shared ground-truth associations, rigid-aligned translation RMSE was **0.165 m stereo versus 0.395 m stereo–IMU**. Independent direct numerical recomputation reproduced both values. Neither an inertial improvement nor continuous inertial tracking is established. The [report and measured figure](TUMVI_EXTERNAL_VALIDATION_2026-10-11.md) retain the reset, rejected initialization frames, latency, calibration and evaluator correction. Validation on the user's robot remains blocked on access.

The completed occlusion study provides a controlled negative result for a general fusion improvement claim. Under heavy occlusion, the FoundationStereo local-cost gate reduced false-free obstacle pixels by 62.0%, but coverage dropped from 96.12% to 61.78% and obstacle-pixel recall from 91.82% to 69.97%. On shared valid pixels, MAE changed by only 0.287 mm: most of the native-support error reduction comes from abstention. These are descriptive results from two held-out seed clusters, not navigation or safety outcomes. [Measured figures, full denominators and raw-data verification](FUSION_OCCLUSION_CONTROLS_2026-10-11.md).

The first occlusion attempt stopped before image rendering or model inference because protocol serialization changed dictionary iteration order. Explicit phase ordering and a serialization regression test corrected that bookkeeping defect. No outcomes were used to choose the geometry, seeds or method thresholds.

[GitHub CI for implementation commit 402d577](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/actions/runs/38130808419) passed **2,887 tests**, with 132 skips and 10 subtests, plus actual trained-asset checks and seeded physics/fault replay. Test counts differ from the HPC environment because available native dependencies differ; skips are not claimed as passes.

New raw outputs and frozen sources use HPC share and node-local scratch. Dispatchers preserve at least 8 GiB of shared free space, reserve bounded output capacity and verify freshly downloaded published bytes before retiring local duplicates. The completed older home-directory campaigns were separately hash-verified and migrated with compatibility links; the methods source root was preserved because its original guards depend on that path.

No measured development gain is upgraded to a confirmation or physical claim until the corresponding complete evidence passes its declared gate. Smoke failures, uninitialized estimators, failed qualification and missing physical access remain visible results or blockers.
